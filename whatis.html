Why it works.

Word of mouth is the highest-converting channel in B2B and the only one nobody can invoice for. A buyer hears your name from someone they already trust, and by the time they reach your website the decision is half made.

The problem was never that it doesn't work. The problem is that it arrives by accident, and anything that arrives by accident cannot be planned, budgeted, or defended in a board meeting. So it gets treated as luck, and the budget goes to the channel with the neater dashboard.

We treat it as a system instead. Every relationship worth having in Nordic fintech already belongs to someone — a partner, a community, a newsletter, a person who convenes people. Building an audience by proxy means borrowing that standing rather than buying attention from strangers.

In practice that means co-hosting with adjacent companies, splitting the guest list, and following up inside forty-eight hours while recall is intact. You inherit an audience that already trusts the partner, and you keep every contact it produces.

And it is countable. Who was invited, who registered, who attended, who engaged afterwards, and which of your named target accounts moved from stranger to conversation. Every one of those is a record, and every record maps back to the buying committee we identified in week one.

What you end up with is not a spike in impressions. It is a list of people with names, employers, and a reason to take your call — which is the only version of word of mouth you can put on a slide.

FAQ

What is B2B marketing?

B2B marketing is the work of getting a group of people to trust a company enough to buy from it. In fintech that group is six to ten people across product, engineering, finance, risk, compliance and the executive team. Almost none of them can be reached with advertising, and most of them form their opinion of you from someone they already trust rather than from anything you published.

Why is word of mouth so hard to measure?

Because the moment that matters happens without you. Someone recommends you in a meeting you are not in, and the effect surfaces weeks later as an inbound enquiry with no traceable source. The channel that converts best is the one that shows up in your analytics as direct traffic, which is why it gets cut first when budgets tighten.

What does audience by proxy mean?

Every relationship worth having in Nordic fintech already belongs to someone — a partner, a community, a newsletter, someone who convenes people. Audience by proxy means borrowing that standing by co-hosting with them, rather than buying attention from strangers. You inherit their credibility for an evening and keep the contacts permanently.

So how do you make relationships measurable?

By fixing the points where a relationship becomes a record. Who was invited, who registered, who attended, who engaged with the follow-up, and which named target accounts moved from stranger to conversation. That is a countable pipeline made of people rather than impressions, and it is checked against the buying committee you mapped in week one.

Does this replace advertising entirely?

For a Nordic fintech selling to a few hundred named people, largely yes — that is what the eight-week term is built on. If your buyer is spread across twenty countries, or you need thousands of leads rather than dozens of correct ones, paid media and SEO are the honest answer and you should spend the budget there instead.
