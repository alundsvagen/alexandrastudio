Why Fintech Growth Is Different

And why most B2B marketing gets it backwards.

Fintech is not an attention problem.

You probably don't need 100,000 people to know who you are.
You need the right 100.

A fintech sale can involve a founder, product leader, engineer, compliance, finance, procurement and an executive sponsor. They talk to each other. They compare notes. They ask someone they trust whether you're any good.

Which means the usual B2B playbook — buy attention, generate leads, measure clicks — is often built around the wrong unit.

The unit that matters is the relationship.

The old B2B playbook breaks down

Buy attention

Ads can reach a lot of people. They don't necessarily reach the six people who need to agree to buy.

Generate leads

A form fill is not a relationship. And a database full of contacts is not a buying committee.

Prove ROI

When the real conversion happens in a meeting, a WhatsApp thread or an introduction, the dashboard misses the part that mattered.

So we build the thing that actually works.

Fintech growth is largely a relationship game.
The mistake is treating that as a reason to give up on measurement.

We do the opposite.
We make relationship-building deliberate.

Find the people who matter.
Find the people they already trust.
Create a reason for them to meet.
Give them something worth talking about.
Follow up.
Keep the relationship.
Measure what changed.

That is marketing you can actually defend.

What relationship-led growth looks like

Borrow Trust

Your buyers already have trusted relationships with other companies, communities and people in the ecosystem. We find the ones who are adjacent to you — not competing with you — and build with them.

Create the Room

A useful room gives people a reason to show up. We design the topic, invite the right people, work with partners who can extend the reach, and make the room worth attending.

Keep the Relationship

The event is not the outcome. The relationship is. Registrations, attendance, engagement and follow-up become a record against named accounts and people — so the value compounds after the room is over.

From room to pipeline.

You get into the room with people who matter. Then the content layer takes the room beyond the room — so the conversation reaches people who weren't there.

See what it produced

The market is smaller than it looks.

Nordic fintech is not a mass market. A relatively small group of people influence a very large number of buying decisions.

They move between companies. They sit on advisory boards. They attend the same events. They introduce each other. They share suppliers.

They already have a network. So why would you try to build an audience from scratch?

The better question is: whose trust can you earn?

A good partner gives you more than distribution. They give you context. They give you credibility. They give people a reason to listen.

And once the relationship exists, it doesn't disappear when the campaign ends.

That is why we care about rooms, relationships and content in that order.

The room creates the relationship.
The relationship creates the story.
The story creates the next room.

FAQ

What is relationship-led growth?

Growth built around the people who influence a buying decision rather than around anonymous attention. For fintech, that usually means identifying the buying committee, understanding who already has their trust, creating useful reasons for those people to meet, and building a system around the relationships that follow.

Isn't this just event marketing?

No. The room is the starting point, not the product. The work continues through follow-up, content, distribution, partner relationships and account-level tracking. The event creates the conditions for relationships; the system turns those relationships into something that compounds.

How do you measure word of mouth?

We measure the moments where a relationship becomes visible: who was invited, who registered, who attended, who engaged afterwards, which target accounts were reached and which moved into conversation.

Does this replace paid advertising?

Not always. If you need to reach millions of people, paid media can make sense. If you sell a complex fintech product to a few hundred relevant people, we would rather know who those people are and build relationships with them.
