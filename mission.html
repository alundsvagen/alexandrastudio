Our vision

Collaboration is the source of growth

The bet

A few hundred people decide most of what happens in Nordic fintech. In a market that size, the companies that grow fastest are not the ones who shout loudest — they are the ones who are structurally present: on the invitation, in the room, mentioned by the partner, quoted in the recap.

Ecosystem collaboration is the best growth hack available in B2B fintech, and it is chronically under-used because it does not fit neatly into a channel budget.

What we do differently

  • We sell an outcome, publicly. 30 qualified leads and 5 held calls in eight weeks, with the definitions written down before we start. If it isn't met, the invoice for weeks five to eight is cancelled.
  • One client per vertical. You never share a room, a partner or my attention with a competitor.
  • You own the assets. Guest lists, subscribers, partner relationships, content. They keep working after the engagement ends.
  • We say when we're the wrong answer. If your buyer isn't reachable in the Nordics, or you need thousands of leads rather than dozens of correct ones, ads or a hire is the honest recommendation.

Questions people ask before the call

Why one client per vertical?

Because the asset being shared is a room, and you cannot put two competitors on the same invitation without devaluing it for both. It caps how many engagements run at once, which is a deliberate constraint rather than a scarcity tactic.

Who is Alexandra Grisanti?

A B2B growth hacker and fractional CMO for European fintechs, based in Stockholm and originally from the United States. She runs Alexandra Studio, the Operator Network — an application-only C-suite community — and the Stockholm Growth & Fintech newsletter. She came into growth through design and UX research, including two years building fintech product at Zwapgrid, which is why the work starts with the buyer rather than the channel.

Who actually does the work?

Alexandra, with some help. No handover to a junior, and no pooled team shared across accounts.

What do we keep when the engagement ends?

Guest lists, the newsletter and its subscribers, partner relationships, and every piece of content produced. The point of the model is that the assets do not stop working when the invoicing does.
