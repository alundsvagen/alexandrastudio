Alexandra Studio's vision is about earning a seat at the table. In B2B fintech you rarely win a customer through advertising; you win because, when the conversation starts, the buyer already knows who you are — through the right introductions, rooms and events, clear positioning, useful content that keeps you top of mind, and a real understanding of the buyer's problems and objections. Our Vision

How to earn a seat at the table.

You don't win a customer because someone saw your ad three times.

You win because when the conversation starts, they already know who you are.

They know what you do.
They remember something you said.
Someone they trust has mentioned you.
They understand the problem you solve.

So when the opportunity comes up, you're not a stranger trying to get in.
You're already at the table.

The hardest part is getting the introduction.

Most markets are crowded. Especially fintech.

Your prospects already have suppliers. They already have opinions. They already have people they trust.

The challenge isn't simply explaining why you're good.
It's getting the chance to explain it.

The right introduction changes everything. So does being in the right room. Going to the right event. Knowing the right people. Being remembered for something useful.

Once people know you, the conversation is different.

You get about 30 seconds. Make them count.

Someone introduces you.
A prospect asks what you do.
You have a moment to explain why you are different.

That's where brand matters.

Not the logo. Not the colour palette. Not a clever tagline.

What does this person understand about you before they ever become a customer?

What are you good at?
Where are you genuinely different?
What problem do you understand better than most?

And, importantly, what are you not good at?

Good positioning makes that obvious.

Stay known before you're needed.

Most B2B buyers aren't ready to buy when they first meet you. That's okay.

The job isn't always to close them today. Sometimes the job is simply to give them a reason to remember you.

A useful idea.
A sharp observation.
A conversation worth joining.
Something they read and think, “That's exactly the problem we're dealing with.”

That's what good content does. It keeps you close to the conversation until the timing is right.

The newsletter isn't there to manufacture leads. It's there to stay top of mind.

Know what they care about.

You cannot be memorable to someone you don't understand.

Know what they're trying to solve.
Know what they're worried about.
Know what keeps coming up in their conversations.
Know what changes with the market, the quarter, the regulation, the budget cycle.

Then talk about that.

When people feel like you understand what they're dealing with, they stay and listen. You don't have to shout louder when you're saying something that feels relevant.

Work in their shoes.

Objections usually come from the same place as the buying decision: uncertainty.

Is this worth it?
Will it actually work?
Why should we change?
Why you?

Good marketing doesn't try to talk someone out of those questions. It understands them.

It says: “I know why you're hesitating. I've seen this before. Here's what is actually happening — and here's what you can do about it.”

Then show them.
Show the numbers.
Show the trade-off.
Show how you'd approach it.

Don't force the sale.

No tricks. No shortcuts.

Sales gimmicks can create short-term gains. But if someone has to be manipulated into becoming your customer, you've probably created the wrong customer.

Shortcuts don't build trust.
Tricks don't build relationships.
And pretending you're something you're not is a terrible foundation for a long sales cycle.

People are getting better at spotting it. They're more selective. They have more information. They can ask their network. They can compare you with five alternatives before they ever take a call.

So earn the trust instead.

Build the product.
Know the customer.
Be useful.
Show your work.
Build the relationships.

And when you get a seat at the table, be good enough to win it.

Our job is to get you a seat at the table.
Once you're there, you should be good enough to win.

Come and say hello.

If any of this sounds like the way you already think about growth, I would like to hear what you are building.

FAQ

Why only fintech?

Because the problems are real and specifically hard. Getting an SME paid on time, lending to a company a bank will not touch, moving money across a border without a week of reconciliation — these are load-bearing problems for the businesses that have them. Working in one category also means we already know who your buyer is, which partners are worth co-hosting with, and what has been tried.

Why one client per vertical?

Because the asset being shared is a room, and you cannot put two competitors on the same invitation without devaluing it for both. It caps how many engagements run at once, which is a deliberate constraint rather than a scarcity tactic.

Who actually does the work?

Alexandra, with some help. No handover to a junior, and no pooled team shared across accounts.

What do we keep when the engagement ends?

Guest lists, the newsletter and its subscribers, partner relationships, and every piece of content produced. The point of the model is that the assets keep working after the invoicing stops.
