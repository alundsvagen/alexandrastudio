What we believe

The Problems Are Worth Solving

There is no shortage of them left. Payments, lending, compliance and reconciliation are still full of work that has not been done, and the companies doing it deserve to be understood by the market rather than out-marketed by whoever bought the most ads.

Collaboration Beats Competition

Funding is getting harder for smaller players, and competing for attention against better-funded companies is a fight you lose slowly. Co-hosting is the alternative: two companies who share a buyer put their names on one invitation and both leave with more than they arrived with.

Depth First, Then New Markets

Traction deep inside one market travels. Become the name the Nordic ecosystem associates with your category, and the introduction into the next market comes from someone who already trusts you — which is a far cheaper way to expand than starting from zero.

Why it works.

Investment has got more competitive for smaller players. Rounds are slower, the bar is higher, and the marketing budget is usually the first thing that gets defended rather than grown. In that environment, outspending anyone is not a strategy — and the companies who try it end up buying attention they cannot afford to keep buying.

What is still cheap is other people's credibility. Every company selling into fintech already has relationships with the same buyers you want, and most of them are not your competitor. A partner who co-hosts with you brings a room you would have spent a quarter building, and takes one from you in return. Nobody loses anything they had.

That is the whole bet: collaboration and word of mouth are the two levers that still work when budgets do not.

It also matches how this market actually behaves. A few hundred people decide most of what happens in Nordic fintech, and they talk to each other constantly. A name that keeps appearing on invitations alongside credible companies becomes credible itself, faster than any campaign could manage.

And depth is what makes expansion possible later. A company that is genuinely known in Stockholm does not enter Helsinki or Amsterdam as a stranger — it enters through someone who vouches for it. Traction in one market is not a smaller version of international growth. It is the thing that makes international growth affordable.

There is far more still to solve in this industry than there are companies solving it. Doing it together is simply the faster route.

FAQ

Why only fintech?

Because the problems are real and specifically hard. Getting an SME paid on time, lending to a company a bank will not touch, moving money across a border without a week of reconciliation — these are load-bearing problems for the businesses that have them. Working in one category also means we already know who your buyer is, which partners are worth co-hosting with, and what has been tried.

Why one client per vertical?

Because the asset being shared is a room, and you cannot put two competitors on the same invitation without devaluing it for both. It caps how many engagements run at once, which is a deliberate constraint rather than a scarcity tactic.

Who actually does the work?

Alexandra, with some help. No handover to a junior, and no pooled team shared across accounts.

What do we keep when the engagement ends?

Guest lists, the newsletter and its subscribers, partner relationships, and every piece of content produced. The point of the model is that the assets keep working after the invoicing stops.
