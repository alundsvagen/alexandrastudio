About the founder

Who is Alexandra Grisanti?

The founder of Alexandra Studio, the Operator Network and the Stockholm Growth & Fintech newsletter: her background, what she has done in Nordic fintech, how the three brands fit together, and who actually does the work.

By Alexandra Grisanti Published 22 October 2026 Updated 22 October 2026 10 min read

The short answer

Alexandra Grisanti is a Stockholm-based founder who runs Alexandra Studio, a B2B growth studio that works only with fintech companies. She also runs the Operator Network, a community and event stage for fintech and tech executives, and publishes the Stockholm Growth & Fintech newsletter.

She is American, moved to Sweden in 2021, lives on Södermalm and holds a Master of Architecture in landscape architecture. Before starting Alexandra Studio she worked in UX research, product design and service design for Nordic fintechs, then as an ad designer at the Stockholm agency Heijbel Growth. In 2026 she spoke at Stockholm Fintech Week, hosted the Nordic Fintech Highlights Stage at Nordic Fintech Summit in Helsinki, and designed and ran eleven in-person B2B gatherings for clients including Heijbel Growth, Solvapay and Cardboard. Outside work, she organises a 127-member beach volleyball group on Gärdet in Stockholm every summer.

How long has the business existed?

Not long, and we would rather say so plainly. The newsletter is from December 2025. The studio's client events all took place in 2026. What is older is the fintech experience: Alexandra has worked on Nordic fintech products since 2022, starting inside Zwapgrid's product team.

What a design background brings to B2B fintech growth

People researching Alexandra often notice that her testimonials talk about product, design and websites, and that she has no background in bank sales. Both are true. Here is why the design background is the foundation of the studio rather than a detour from it.

Architecture: designing the room

Landscape architecture is about designing spaces around how people actually move, gather and stay. Alexandra Studio's core product is a designed room: who sits at the table, which question they come to answer, which co-host makes the invitation feel natural, and why a busy executive would give up a morning for it.

UX research: mapping who decides

At Zwapgrid, Alexandra interviewed users and mapped how decisions were made around B2B financial software. A fintech sale to a bank involves six to ten people across leadership, finance, product, engineering, compliance and procurement. Mapping that buying committee, and working out what each person needs to see, is the same research skill pointed at a sales problem.

Product and service design: white-label finance

Her design work at Zwapgrid and Monitum was on white-label products: credit, identity and fraud prevention, and B2B integrations that financial-services companies deploy under their own brand. That is the world many of our clients sell into, and it is why conversations about APIs, sandboxes and partner distribution are familiar rather than foreign.

Ad design and the CMO breakfasts: what makes people show up

At Heijbel Growth she learned what makes a busy reader stop scrolling, and started the CMO breakfast series that became the model for Alexandra Studio: a small group of senior peers, one sharp question, and brands they already trust in the room.

What she does not have

Alexandra has not worked as a bank salesperson or in enterprise business development at a bank, and Alexandra Studio does not sell a personal address book. What the studio sells is a repeatable way to give the right people a reason to be in the same room. How Alexandra Studio works sets out which banks and institutions we have already been in rooms with, and how we approach the ones we haven't.

Alexandra Studio, the Operator Network and Stockholm Growth & Fintech: three brands, one founder

Proposals sometimes read "Alexandra Studio × The Operator Network", which can look like two firms or a larger team. It is neither. All three are run by Alexandra, and each has one job.

BrandWhat it isIts jobDo clients pay for it?
Alexandra Studio The B2B growth studio: rooms, series, build days and advisory for fintech clients, hosted under the client's name. The service Yes. This is what you contract with.
The Operator Network A curated community for founders and C-suite executives in Stockholm's fintech and tech scene, with speaker invitations, panels and breakfasts. The stage: how invitations go out and how executives get a platform No. Membership is by application and is not sold to clients.
Stockholm Growth & Fintech A newsletter on Stockholm fintech and B2B growth, with a podcast featuring fintech companies planned next. The trust: why people take our invitations seriously No. It is free to read.

In practice: you sign with Alexandra Studio (A. Grisanti Ventures). Your company hosts the event under its own name, usually with a co-host your buyers already trust. The Operator Network appears, if at all, as "in partnership with", because that is where many of the invitations and speakers come from. The rule we work by is short: sell the room, never sell the network.

Who does the work?

Alexandra Studio is founder-led and deliberately small. Alexandra personally leads every engagement:

  • the strategy, and mapping your buying committee and target accounts;
  • the guest list, the invitations and the co-host conversations;
  • the topic, the speakers and hosting or moderating on the day;
  • the follow-up report on who came, from which accounts, and what happened next.

For larger events, production support such as venue logistics, catering and on-the-day help is contracted per event. You are not handed to a junior team, because there isn't one. That is also why the studio works with one client per vertical at a time: it protects your relationships and ideas, and it caps how much work Alexandra takes on.

Outside fintech: beach volleyball on Gärdet

Every summer, Alexandra organises a beach volleyball group on Gärdet in Stockholm. It has 127 members, and once a summer the group throws a midnight volleyball party.

It is the same skill as the day job, minus the lanyards: pick a place, a time and a reason to show up, then keep doing it until people come back on their own. The relationships take care of themselves after that.
