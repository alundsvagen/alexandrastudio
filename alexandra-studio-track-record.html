Track record

Every room we've run, and what it produced

Eleven in-person B2B gatherings in 2026, with dates, clients, co-hosts and results, and a plain account of which numbers are counted and which are estimates.

By Alexandra Grisanti Published 22 October 2026 Updated 22 October 2026 10 min read

The short answer

Between February and September 2026, Alexandra Studio designed and ran eleven in-person B2B gatherings in Stockholm, Gothenburg and Amsterdam: three CMO breakfasts and an industry panel for the agency Heijbel Growth; three builder nights and two partner dinners for the Stockholm fintech Solvapay; the Embedded Finance Build Day, a one-day hackathon with Open Payments, Zwapgrid, Danske Bank, Froda, Worldline and Dun & Bradstreet; and a CEO + CFO breakfast for Cardboard. Alexandra also hosted the Nordic Fintech Highlights Stage at Nordic Fintech Summit 2026 in Helsinki.

The best-documented result is the Build Day: SEK 25,000 spent, 26 companies in the room, 44 builders, and 14 warm leads at SEK 1,785 each, with no paid advertising. Pipeline values are estimates and are labelled as estimates. Booked revenue belongs to our clients and is reported to them, not published.

Heijbel Growth: a CMO breakfast series and an industry panel

Client
Heijbel Growth, a marketing agency on Södermalm, Stockholm
Audience
CMOs at companies with 50–300 people
What we built
Three CMO breakfasts, plus an industry panel at Vitalis 2026 in Gothenburg with Natural Cycles, Mindler and Sleep Cycle, which Alexandra Studio pitched and cast, followed by a dinner for the panelists

Each breakfast was a small morning round for senior marketers around one theme, co-hosted with brands the audience already knew: Voxo and Sitoo at the first, Legora at the second, Mindler and Natural Cycles at the third.

What it opened: repeat, peer-level access to the CMO audience the agency sells to, and two new prospects for the agency, Natural Cycles and Mindler, brought onto the stage as speakers rather than approached cold.

This series is the origin of the studio's model. Alexandra started the CMO breakfasts while working at Heijbel Growth as an ad designer; her background explains how it grew from there.

Solvapay: builder nights and partner dinners

Client
Solvapay, an early-stage, Antler-backed fintech in Kungsträdgården, Stockholm, building payments infrastructure for agentic commerce. It raised a €2.4m pre-seed round in April 2026.
Goal
B2B traction of any kind, and distribution through partnerships
What we built
Three builder nights, two partner dinners (one in Amsterdam), and the Embedded Finance Build Day with ecosystem partners

Agentic payments is a young market. For a company less than a year old, go-to-market means education, brand and trust at the same time, so the builder nights put Solvapay's MCP server in front of developers and partners who could build on it in the room. The two documented builder nights each drew 30+ registrations.

What it opened: AndSend, Open Payments, Visa, Cardboard and Kindly came out of the series as potential customers for Solvapay. The two partner dinners gave named access to the payments and fintech ecosystem, with partner decision-makers around one table and time to talk. The second dinner travelled to Amsterdam in June, after Money20/20 Europe, with Copenhagen Fintech, Swedish Fintech, Surfboard Payments, Movitz Payments, Narvi Payments and Griffin Bank at the table.

Every team used both partner APIs. Seven teams independently chose the same fraud problem, and three built the same missing building block: product feedback the API partners could not have bought from a survey.

Fifteen target accounts engaged with the event without registering, including SEB Embedded, Fortnox, Klarna and Insurely. We count these as reach, not as leads: people from those companies saw and engaged with the event, but they did not attend and did not opt in. That distinction matters, and we keep it.

It applies an assumed conversion rate of about 20% to the warm leads, which gives two to three potential new clients, and values those clients at roughly SEK 1.5 million in total. The 20% rate is an assumption about in-person events in general, not a rate we have measured. It is not booked revenue, and we don't present it as such.

How we measure and report results

Attendance is where measurement starts, not where it ends. Every series is reported against the same eight-line scorecard, from who actually showed up to revenue booked:

#MeasureWhat it meansType
1Verified attendanceUnique people who actually checked in. Not invites, not sign-ups.Counted
2Target-account coverageShare of your named target companies represented in the room.Counted
3Buying-committee depthTarget accounts with two or more engaged people.Counted
4Qualified conversationsMeetings with a defined need, the right role and an agreed next step.Counted
5Meetings bookedFollow-up meetings booked within 30 days of the event.Counted
6PipelineOpportunities sourced and influenced, reported separately.Client's CRM
7Stage progressionDeals advancing a stage within 30, 60 and 90 days.Client's CRM
8RevenueClosed-won deals and booked revenue.Client's CRM

Three rules for our numbers

  1. Counts and estimates are never mixed. Leading indicators such as attendance, coverage and conversations are always reported separately from pipeline and booked revenue. Any estimate is labelled, with the assumption behind it.
  2. A qualified lead is a named person who opted in. Someone at a company matching your ICP who registered for an event, subscribed to your newsletter or replied to outreach, and whose contact details you keep. Not a scraped list, not an anonymous form fill, and not someone who only reacted to a post.
  3. Booked revenue belongs to the client. We report it to you in the follow-up and publish it only with your permission. That is why this page shows what we can count ourselves, not our clients' closed deals.

A note on ROI figures

Our case-study page used to lead with a single ROI percentage for the Build Day. That figure combined counted results with estimated values, namely the potential pipeline above and an ad-equivalent value for organic reach, so it couldn't be reproduced from counts alone. The case-study page now reports counts and estimates separately, as this page does, and we treat any ROI percentage as an estimate.

How every engagement runs now

Our first year taught us as much from what was hard as from what went well. Each rule below fixes something a client or partner told us, and each is now standard:

  1. A written room agreement at kickoff: brand order, who invites whom, who pays for what, the hospitality level, and what success means.
  2. A five-line update every Friday until the event, so nobody has to ask what is happening.
  3. The client is the host on the invitation. Alexandra Studio is the producer. We never call a client a sponsor, on stage or off.
  4. Hospitality agreed in writing, from a simple buffet to white-glove service, so expectations match before the day.
  5. A briefed stand-in with a script if Alexandra can't be there in person.
  6. Feedback within seven days: we ask every client for a quote and a reference call after the event.

Questions people ask about our track record

How many events has Alexandra Studio run?

Eleven in-person B2B gatherings between February and September 2026: three CMO breakfasts and an industry panel for Heijbel Growth, three builder nights and two partner dinners for Solvapay, the Embedded Finance Build Day, and a CEO + CFO breakfast for Cardboard. Alexandra Grisanti also hosted the Nordic Fintech Highlights Stage at Nordic Fintech Summit 2026 in Helsinki for Helsinki Fintech Farm.

What did the Embedded Finance Build Day produce?

The Build Day on 25 August 2026 in Stockholm cost SEK 25,000 and used no paid advertising. It had 98 registrations and 72 approved places; 44 builders formed 15 teams and built 15 working prototypes; 26 companies attended; and it produced 14 warm leads, a cost of SEK 1,785 per warm lead. 142 organisations were reached organically, including 15 target accounts such as SEB Embedded, Fortnox, Klarna and Insurely whose people engaged without registering.

Is the SEK 1.5 million pipeline figure booked revenue?

No. It is an estimate. The Build Day report applied an assumed conversion rate of about 20 percent to the warm leads, which gives two to three potential new clients, and valued those clients at roughly SEK 1.5 million in total. The 20 percent rate is an assumption, not a measured result. Alexandra Studio reports estimates separately from counted results and from booked revenue.

Who have Alexandra Studio's clients been?

Event clients in 2026 were Heijbel Growth, a Stockholm marketing agency; Solvapay, an early-stage Stockholm fintech building payments for agentic commerce; and Cardboard, a Norwegian company entering Sweden. Alexandra also worked with Helsinki Fintech Farm on the Nordic Fintech Highlights newsletter and stage. Ecosystem partners at the events included Open Payments, Zwapgrid, Danske Bank, Froda, Worldline, Visa and Dun & Bradstreet.

Why doesn't Alexandra Studio publish booked revenue for clients?

Closed deals belong to the client. Alexandra Studio reports them to the client in its follow-up reporting and publishes them only with the client's permission. Public figures are limited to what the studio can count itself, such as registrations, check-ins, companies in the room and leads, plus clearly labelled estimates.
