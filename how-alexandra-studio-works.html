What Alexandra Studio does

A fintech sale to a bank or a licensed institution usually involves six to ten people across leadership, finance, product, engineering, compliance and procurement. Most of them won't be reached by an ad, and they don't take cold meetings from vendors they haven't heard of. They do turn up for a well-chosen room: a breakfast with their peers, a panel, a dinner, a build day on an API they're curious about.

So the work runs in five steps:

  1. You name the accounts. We start from 30–50 target companies and the roles on each buying committee.
  2. We design the room. One sharp question, the right size, the right format: breakfast, dinner, panel, builder night or build day.
  3. We bring the co-host. A brand your buyers already trust, so the invitation feels natural rather than like a sales pitch.
  4. We run the invitations. Personal invitations to named people, through our network, the Operator Network and the co-host's.
  5. We report who came, by name, and which of your target accounts were covered, then track follow-up meetings for 30 days.

An agency makes content. A booth buys presence. We buy you time with the accounts you named.

Who it's for, and who it isn't

A good fit

  • A B2B fintech of roughly 30–500 people
  • Selling to banks, PSPs, EMIs or other licensed buyers, or entering a new Nordic market
  • Series A or later, or profitable
  • A sales or partnerships champion, and a marketing owner with budget
  • Can name its targets: a tier-1 bank, an open RFP, a new market

Probably not yet

  • Under about 15 people, or pre-launch
  • Growing well by word of mouth and not looking for new pipeline
  • Nobody can name a target account
  • No budget owner will join a second call

If you're in the second column, we'll say so on the first call, and point you to something more useful for now, such as the newsletter or a panel seat.

What this means in practice

We have warm routes into some banks and none yet into others. We would rather show you that table than imply an address book we don't have. What we sell is the method for getting into the rooms where bank people already are, and for building new rooms they will want to attend.

How we reach a bank we don't know yet

Banks don't respond to cold vendor pitches; in the Nordics, you need warm introductions. So we create the warm part:

  1. Borrow trust. Co-host with a brand the bank already works with: one of your partners, or one of ours. Cardboard, a Norwegian company with no Swedish network, was positioned in Stockholm next to Gilion and Fairlo for exactly this reason.
  2. Give a stage before asking for anything. A speaker invitation, a seat on a conference panel, a newsletter or podcast feature. People who have been offered a stage take the next meeting.
  3. Find the right person inside the bank. Usually the people who connect business goals with digital delivery, not the most senior name on the website.
  4. Set a go/no-go date. You see who has confirmed before the event goes ahead. If the room isn't right, we move the date rather than run a thin event.

Before you sign, the proposal names the role we would approach at each of your target banks, the route in (a co-host, a stage, an introduction or a cold invitation), and whether that route already exists or has to be built. If we can't name a credible route for a bank on your list, the proposal says so.

Whose name is on the invitation?

Yours. The invitation reads "[Your company] × [Co-host]". Alexandra Studio is the producer, and the Operator Network appears, if at all, as "in partnership with". We never call a client a sponsor, on stage or off. The brand order is agreed in writing at kickoff.

What you keep

The relationships, the list of opted-in contacts, and any content made from the event. We work with one client per vertical at a time, so the relationships and ideas we build with you aren't reused for a competitor.

What to ask before you sign

If you're evaluating a proposal from us, these are fair questions, and we would rather you ask them than wonder:

  1. Which route do you have into each of our target banks? The proposal should name the role, the route and whether it exists yet.
  2. Which guarantee applies to this proposal? Pay-only-when-it-works on the growth engagement, a go/no-go date on a single project, or an outcome-linked element you've agreed.
  3. Who does the work? Alexandra personally, with production support per event. See who does the work.
  4. What's in the cost estimate? Venue, food and travel, itemised, approved by you before anything is booked.
  5. Can we speak to a past client? Yes. We'll arrange a reference call on request.
  6. What will you report, and when? Verified attendance and target-account coverage after the event, and meetings booked within 30 days.

What does Alexandra Studio cost?

The growth engagement is free for the first month. After that it costs SEK 55,000 per month excluding VAT, invoiced only for months in which 30 qualified leads and 5 held calls are reached, with no ad spend on top; event costs are quoted separately, approved before anything is booked and billed at cost. Does Alexandra Studio offer a guarantee?

Yes. On the growth engagement the fee itself is the guarantee: the first month is free, and after that the SEK 55,000 monthly fee (excluding VAT) is invoiced only for months in which 30 qualified leads and 5 held calls are reached. It runs month to month, so the client can stop at any time and keeps everything produced. Single projects include a go/no-go date: the client sees the confirmed guest list before the event, and if it is short the date moves at no cost. Advisory has no attendance guarantee because the client runs the invitations; an outcome-linked element can be agreed in writing before signing.

How much time does my team need to give?

For a single room: three 30-minute calls, one guest-list review and the day itself. The client also names 30 to 50 target accounts at the start and follows up with the people they meet afterwards. Alexandra Studio does the rest, from co-host and invitations to production and the follow-up report.

Will my company be called a sponsor?

No. The client is the host. The invitation reads as the client together with its co-host, Alexandra Studio is the producer, and the Operator Network appears only as "in partnership with". The brand order is agreed in writing at kickoff.

Does Alexandra Studio work with competing fintechs?

No. Alexandra Studio works with one client per vertical at a time, which protects each client's relationships and ideas and limits how many engagements the founder takes on.
